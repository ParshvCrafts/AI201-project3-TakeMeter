# Reading notes (Milestone 1)

**Community:** r/LocalLLaMA, the subreddit for people running language models
on their own hardware. It's public, it's busy, and the comments range from
careful benchmark write-ups to one-line gripes. It also isn't built around one
show or release. Model launches come and go, but the sub talks about all of
them.

**How I read it:** I pulled about 260 comments from four days spread across
2026 (March, May, July, September) and read 40 of them start to finish.

## What I noticed

1. **Some posts cite a checkable outside fact. Most don't.** "GLM-5 training
   began in December 2025. The researchers can't pull data from the future."
   That's a date you could look up, and the whole point rests on it.

2. **Some posts report what the writer actually ran and what happened.** "I
   asked gpt oss to output the full reasoning of earlier turns exactly, word
   for word, and it was able to do that." Nobody else could have written that
   sentence. It's a result from their own machine.

3. **A lot of posts are confident claims with nothing behind them.** "OpenAI
   should be held legally accountable." "this is just a pedantic nerd
   argument... it's COMMON SENSE."

4. **Many posts are questions.** "this model looks like it's a little too small
   for a macbook air m4 24gb of ram, right?" The writer wants an answer, not
   to make a point.

## Things I'll need to filter out

- Bot posts (the "your post is getting popular, we featured it on our
  Discord" bot showed up twice in 40).
- Referral spam ("use my link").
- Posts that are mostly a quote of someone else's comment.
- Very short replies like "Nop, I didn't back then." There's nothing to label.

## The one that already worried me

"Both 27B and 35B v3.5 models seem to be more focused than the v3.6 models.
I've spent countless hours tinkering with params, different quants and quant
providers and I've just settled on the conclusion that 3.5 is just generally
better for me."

It sounds like hands-on experience, but it never says what actually happened
beyond "more focused" and "better". This is where I expect my labels to fight.
